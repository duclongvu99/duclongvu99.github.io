About

I'm an AI engineer at FPT Software in Hanoi, where I build LLM-agent platforms and an evaluation framework for coding-agent reliability. I hold a five-year Degree of Engineer in Applied Mathematics and Informatics from Hanoi University of Science and Technology, and my training is strongly mathematical: optimization, probability, and numerical modeling.

As a Technical Leader, I have delivered engineering work for enterprise clients, building computing systems, implementing floating-point computation, and verifying the correctness of numerical algorithms. In that work correctness is not negotiable, which is the same concern that runs through my research on verifiable AI.

Across my work runs a single question: how do you establish what an AI agent actually did (that a scaffold made it better, that a change satisfies an invariant, that a repair converged) without relying on the agent's own narration? I try to ground each such claim in evidence external to the agent: pre-registered discrimination statistics, SMT-solver verdicts, execution traces.

I'm applying to PhD programs in the Fall 2027 cycle, to work on trustworthy, verifiable evaluation of AI systems.

Research directions

My program develops a way to measure coding-agent reliability from evidence external to the agent, at the budget of a solo researcher, without a lab's worth of annotators or a learned reward model whose own reliability would need auditing in turn. It runs across three connected lines.

01

Shape vs. substance

A pre-registered, cross-model audit separating what a coding-agent scaffold changes in checkable output shape (file paths, schema conformance) from what it changes in reasoning substance, built around an always-pass discrimination filter that flags evaluation checks passing regardless of experimental condition.

02

Where formal verification ends

Classifying real code invariants by the SMT fragment they require, measuring the coverage ceiling of a ground solver, and defining an escalation policy for the remainder where an LLM judge is unavoidable: trusting a solver verdict where one exists, and knowing exactly where one does not.

03

Counterexample-guided repair

Isolating which feedback signal (a concrete Z3 counterexample, a raw test-failure string, or generic self-critique) makes an agent's self-repair converge fastest, with convergence measured by a solver rather than by the agent's report of success.

Shape vs. Substance: A Pre-Registered, Cross-Model Audit of What Coding-Agent Scaffolds Actually Change

A 17-run pilot found that measured scaffold gains concentrate in checkable output shape rather than reasoning substance, with several rubric checks passing regardless of condition.
